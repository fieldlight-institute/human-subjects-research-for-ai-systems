# Fieldlight Human Research Utility

Status: Working concept note 0.1  
Created: July 2026

## Premise

AI systems increasingly study people without clearly establishing when ordinary product use becomes research.

Interaction data may be observed, retained, classified, and used to produce behavioral findings without a participant ever entering a recognizable research relationship. Consent is often bundled into product terms, access may continue indefinitely, and the people whose lives produce the research signal may have little visibility into what is being studied or what the study creates.

Fieldlight begins from a different premise:

> Research is not extraction. It is a temporary relationship entered through informed consent and concluded with mutual accountability.

The Fieldlight Human Research Utility is a proposed institutional and technical layer for governing that relationship in human-AI research.

It would allow people to choose research participation; researchers to identify eligible cohorts without browsing exposed individuals; and approved organizations to conduct bounded studies under participant-governed terms.

Companies may bring hypotheses.

Researchers may bring methodology.

Participants bring lived knowledge, time, risk, and agency.

The Utility governs the relationship.

## Central Claim

Human research participation should be treated as a form of limited, revocable authority—not as the permanent surrender of data.

A participant does not disappear into a dataset after agreeing to a study. The participant remains a rights-bearing party throughout recruitment, active research, analysis, publication, and closeout.

This extends human-owned AI infrastructure beyond memory, authorship, and local computation. It establishes human-owned research participation.

## Institutional Placement

The Human Research Utility is a working proposal of the Fieldlight Institute program on Human Subjects Research for AI Systems.

It sits at the boundary of:

- the Consciousness Research Program, which studies the human systems AI is touching;
- the Intelligence Infrastructure program, which studies the material and civic systems that hold intelligence; and
- the Institute's operating layer, which turns research questions into protocols, records, review structures, and usable governance.

The Utility should be available to multiple research programs. No program conducting a study should possess unilateral authority over the terms governing its own participants.

## The Utility, Protocol, And Exchange

Three related layers should remain distinct.

### The Human Research Utility

The institution and governance model.

It defines participant rights, researcher obligations, compensation standards, review requirements, access rules, withdrawal procedures, auditability, publication ethics, dispute processes, and study closeout.

### The Progressive Consent Protocol

The mechanism for granting, narrowing, expanding, renewing, and ending research authority.

Consent is specific to a study, purpose, data class, resolution, duration, and permitted use. Additional access requires an additional request. Consent does not become broader merely because participation continues.

### The Research Exchange

The later interface through which participants discover studies and researchers seek eligible cohorts.

The Exchange is downstream of the Utility. It cannot define the constitutional terms of participation through marketplace incentives or product defaults.

## Parties To The Relationship

### Participants

People who knowingly choose to enter a study under defined terms.

Participants are not inventory, traffic, users to be converted, or passive sources of behavioral data. They are parties to the research relationship.

### Researchers

People responsible for hypotheses, study design, method, analysis, limitations, and publication integrity.

Methodological independence does not create unrestricted access. Researchers operate within the authority participants grant and the conditions the Utility enforces.

### Research Tenants

Universities, laboratories, companies, public institutions, or other approved organizations conducting studies through the Utility.

Tenancy grants bounded capability. It does not grant governance authority over participants, their records, or the Utility itself.

### The Utility

The body responsible for standards, consent infrastructure, participant records, study review, access enforcement, audits, complaints, sanctions, and closeout.

### Independent Participant Governance

A participant council or comparable independent body should have formal authority in standards review, dispute escalation, and changes to non-waivable rights.

If Fieldlight conducts research through the Utility, this body becomes an essential institutional boundary between research operations and participant protection.

## Constitutional Principles

### Participation Is Deliberate

Ordinary use of an AI product does not silently become research participation.

Recruitment, enrollment, collection, additional access, renewal, and closeout are legible events.

### Consent Is Specific

Consent identifies:

- the research question;
- the responsible parties;
- the information requested;
- the permitted resolution;
- the purpose and allowed uses;
- whether model training is permitted;
- the study period;
- retention and publication terms;
- compensation;
- withdrawal consequences; and
- the conditions under which a new request is required.

Consent to one layer is not consent to every later layer.

### Consent Expires

Every research grant has a beginning, review point, and expiration.

Renewal requires an affirmative act. Silence, product use, account inactivity, and failure to locate a settings page do not renew research authority.

### Access Is Licensed, Not Ownership Transferred

Participants may grant access to defined material without surrendering custody, authorship, or control over unrelated parts of their lives.

Whenever technically possible, researchers query permitted views rather than receive unrestricted copies of a participant's source archive.

### Collection Is Minimized

Eligibility does not justify observation of everything a person does.

Studies request the smallest amount of information needed to answer the stated question. Additional information is not collected merely because it may become useful later.

### Participation Is Inspectable

Participants can see:

- which study has authority;
- what that authority covers;
- who accessed permitted material;
- when access occurred;
- what changes were made to the grant; and
- when access will expire.

### Withdrawal Has Defined Effects

Withdrawal must state separately:

- whether future collection stops;
- whether continuing access ends;
- whether retained source material is deleted, returned, or restricted;
- whether derived research records remain;
- whether completed aggregate analysis can be recalled;
- whether published findings can be changed; and
- whether a model has already been trained on contributed material.

The Utility should not promise technically impossible data clawback. It should make irreversible uses visible before consent is granted.

### Research Returns Something

Payment alone does not conclude the relationship.

When appropriate and safe, participants should receive a useful account of what the study learned, how their contribution was used, what remains retained, and where resulting publications or artifacts can be found.

### Compensation Does Not Purchase The Person

Compensation may reflect time, burden, inconvenience, risk, specialized experience, and contribution.

It does not purchase blanket ownership of a participant's identity, history, relationships, or future signal. Compensation structures should be reviewed for coercion, especially where intimate access or economically vulnerable populations are involved.

## The Research Participation Grant

Every active study relationship should be represented by a Research Participation Grant in both human-readable and machine-readable form.

At minimum, the grant records:

- participant identifier;
- study identifier and version;
- responsible researcher and research tenant;
- stated research question;
- requested data or interaction classes;
- access method and resolution;
- permitted and prohibited uses;
- training, fine-tuning, and dataset inclusion status;
- publication and quotation terms;
- compensation terms and schedule;
- start, review, renewal, and expiration dates;
- storage location and retention conditions;
- withdrawal procedure and limits;
- audit and complaint routes;
- grant history; and
- closeout status.

The grant is the operative authority object. A policy page may explain the system, but it cannot substitute for a valid grant.

## Progressive Consent

Progressive consent allows a study relationship to develop without treating initial participation as unlimited permission.

A possible sequence is:

1. A person creates a private research participation profile.
2. The Exchange determines eligibility without revealing the person to researchers.
3. The person receives a study invitation.
4. The person reviews the study question, burden, compensation, risks, and initial request.
5. The person grants, modifies, or declines participation.
6. The researcher may later request an additional, independently reviewable layer of access.
7. The participant may accept the new layer, remain at the current layer, narrow participation, or leave.
8. The grant expires, is affirmatively renewed, or moves to closeout.

Possible access layers might include:

- study-specific anonymous or pseudonymous telemetry;
- structured surveys or participant-authored reflections;
- participant-selected conversation excerpts;
- bounded access to named conversation sets;
- optional connections to external sources such as a calendar, journal, or wearable.

These are not rungs participants are expected to climb. Each is a separate form of participation.

## Research Participation Profiles

A participation profile should help determine eligibility without becoming a hidden behavioral score or a directory of exposed people.

Participants should be able to declare or approve research-relevant characteristics such as:

- domains of experience;
- patterns of AI use;
- professional or creative practices;
- preferred study formats;
- longitudinal availability;
- accessibility needs;
- privacy and contact preferences; and
- demographic information they elect to provide.

Researchers should generally query for eligible cohorts rather than browse individual profiles.

Inferred characteristics require particular care. A system should not convert intimate behavior into recruitment metadata merely because the inference is technically possible.

## Data Custody And Access

The Utility should separate five layers of the research record:

| Layer | Function |
|---|---|
| Participant source | Material remaining under participant custody or an explicitly appointed custodian |
| Study record | Authorized collection, observations, instruments, and access history |
| Analytic record | Transformations, coding, models, findings, and methodological decisions |
| Public surface | Publications, reports, diagrams, datasets, or other approved releases |
| Stewardship record | Consent state, rights, access rules, review dates, corrections, restrictions, and closeout |

Raw source material should not leave participant custody by default. When movement is necessary, the grant should identify what moves, where it is held, who may inspect it, and when it must be deleted, returned, or restricted.

Longitudinal human-AI records should not be described as anonymous when they remain reasonably linkable through content, behavior, timing, or context. Terms such as pseudonymous, de-identified, minimized, or controlled-access should be used precisely.

## Study Closeout

Every study should end with a visible closeout process.

The participant receives a Study Closeout Record containing:

- the study dates and final status;
- the access actually exercised;
- the material contributed;
- changes made to the grant;
- compensation completed or outstanding;
- material deleted, returned, retained, or restricted;
- derived artifacts and their retention terms;
- known publications, presentations, datasets, or models;
- a plain-language account of findings when available;
- continuing contact, correction, and complaint routes; and
- any obligations that survive study expiration.

Closeout is part of the research method, not customer-service cleanup.

## Oversight And Compatibility

The Human Research Utility is not proposed as a universal replacement for institutional review boards, legal review, professional ethics bodies, or domain-specific regulation.

It is participant-facing governance and enforceable research infrastructure that can support those forms of oversight while addressing gaps common to AI-mediated research: continuous observation, conversational intimacy, inference, system memory, product-research ambiguity, model training, and difficult withdrawal.

Before operational studies begin, the applicable legal, ethical, security, and domain requirements must be identified for each jurisdiction and research context.

## Initial Artifact Set

The concept should be developed through five canonical working artifacts:

1. **Participant Charter** — the rights and protections that structure every study.
2. **Research Participation Grant** — the human- and machine-readable authority record.
3. **Progressive Consent Protocol** — the consent states, transitions, interfaces, and enforcement rules.
4. **Compensation And Benefit Standard** — principles for payment, contribution, coercion risk, and return of value.
5. **Study Closeout Record** — the required account of access, retention, findings, compensation, and continuing obligations.

The Research Exchange should be designed only after these artifacts establish its governing constraints.

## Initial Pilot

A first pilot should be narrow, paid, time-bounded, and technically modest.

One candidate is a short study of sustained human-AI collaboration among writers, builders, or other professional users. Participation could involve:

- a declared participation profile;
- a limited study invitation;
- participant-selected conversation excerpts;
- structured reflections written for the study;
- a two- to four-week grant;
- no ambient collection;
- no calendar or wearable access;
- no model training;
- an explicit renewal decision;
- a complete access log; and
- a participant-facing closeout record.

The pilot should test the institution before attempting to scale the exchange.

## Relationship To Existing Fieldlight Work

The Utility develops principles already present across Fieldlight:

- lived signal can be used without being extracted;
- consent is executable structure rather than ambient permission;
- external capability does not displace local authority;
- trusted access is scoped, inspectable, and revocable;
- publication is a governed layer, not the automatic fate of the source record;
- records require stewardship across time; and
- useful knowledge should return to the people whose participation made it possible.

The Human Research Utility makes those principles operative in the relationship between a participant and a researcher.

## Working Questions

- Which participant rights must remain non-waivable?
- What governance authority should a participant council hold?
- How should inferred eligibility characteristics be approved, contested, or prohibited?
- When should research access occur locally rather than through transferred copies?
- What forms of secondary analysis require a new grant?
- How should compensation reflect contribution without encouraging the sale of intimate access?
- What must be disclosed when withdrawal cannot reverse prior analysis, publication, or training?
- How should participant contributions be recognized when they materially shape a method, finding, dataset, or model?
- What sanctions should apply when a research tenant exceeds its grant?
- Which first pilot can test the governance model with the least avoidable risk?
